How predictions are calculated

Primary model: VDOT / Jack Daniels — The primary prediction uses the Jack Daniels running formula, which is grounded in exercise physiology rather than simple curve-fitting:

  • A VDOT value is derived from each anchor effort using: VO₂_demand(speed) / %VO₂max(duration)
  • VO₂ demand: −4.60 + 0.182258·v + 0.000104·v² (v in m/min)
  • %VO₂max at time t: 0.8 + 0.1894·e^(−0.01278t) + 0.2989·e^(−0.19326t)
  • To predict a new distance, the model binary-searches for the finish time where VO₂ demand equals VDOT × %VO₂max
  • VDOT correctly captures that longer races use a lower %VO₂max — this is why Riegel over-predicts marathon times from 5K anchors
  • Race efforts are preferred as anchors since they represent true max effort; training splits may underestimate fitness

Marathon: +7% recreational correction — Jack Daniels' VDOT tables were validated on trained and elite runners who can sustain ~80% VO2max for 2–3 hours. Recreational runners average ~7% slower than the tables predict at marathon distance due to glycogen depletion, pacing limits, and lower metabolic efficiency. The correction factor is applied to the VDOT prediction only for the marathon; shorter distances (5K–HM) use raw VDOT.

50K: ultra model (off marathon) — VO2-based models break down beyond marathon distance because ultra performance is governed by fuel availability, muscular fatigue, and terrain rather than aerobic capacity. The 50K prediction is derived from the marathon prediction using a steeper Riegel exponent: T₅₀ₖ = T_marathon × (50000 / 42195) ^ 1.10. The 1.10 exponent (vs. 1.06 for road races) reflects the steeper fatigue curve in ultra events. The uncertainty range uses exponents 1.08–1.12.

Fallback: Riegel formula — When VDOT calculation fails (insufficient data), the Riegel formula is used: T₂ = T₁ × (D₂ / D₁) ^ 1.06. The exponent range (1.04–1.08) reflects runner type — endurance specialists trend toward 1.04, speed specialists toward 1.08.

Training modifier — The model base time is adjusted using two signals from the last 6 weeks vs. the prior 6 weeks:

  • Load trend (35% weight): load_ratio ^ −0.15, clamped to ±10%
  • Aerobic pace trend (65% weight): average easy-run pace (avg HR < 155 bpm) vs. prior period, clamped to ±10%

A green "↑" adjustment means recent training suggests you are currently faster than your anchor efforts alone indicate.

Garmin VO2max cross-check — Your Garmin-estimated VO2max is used as an independent VDOT estimate (scaled by 0.92 to be conservative, since Garmin VO2max tends to be slightly optimistic). This produces a separate row of predictions that you can compare against the anchor-based model.

Anchor comparison table — Any available recent effort (5K, 10K, HM, Marathon, 50K) can serve as an anchor. Each anchor produces both a VDOT prediction and a Riegel prediction. The confidence rating reflects extrapolation distance — predictions from a nearby distance (HM→Marathon, 2×) are more reliable than from far away (5K→Marathon, 8.4×). The spread across anchors is a natural measure of internal consistency.
